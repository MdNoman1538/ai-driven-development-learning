## What it is

Before optimising, **measure where the time actually goes**: a **benchmark** (time the whole operation, several runs, take the median) and a **profile** (which parts take the time).

## Why it matters

Intuition about performance is often wrong, and optimising the wrong part wastes effort while the real cause stays.

## How we used it

- **The first benchmark changed the architecture.** A 78 ms risk lookup spent about 56 ms just reconnecting to the database. One persistent connection: 15 ms. Holding the coordinates in memory: 0.3 ms. That led to ADR-0003 (precompute, then look up).
- **A wrong diagnosis, corrected.** The status file said large-radius lookups were slow "in Python, not SQL". That claim came from *reading* the code. A profile showed the SQL took 107 of 124 ms. Optimising the Python saved only 11 ms. The status file was corrected, with the measurement.
- **Every claim states its method**: machine, versions, number of runs, median. projectViewer records cold and warm timings (pandas: 4.3 s cold, 1.6 s warm).

<Aside type="tip">
The rule in CLAUDE.md: *"any performance or data claim must come from a measurement you ran; record how."*
</Aside>

## Check yourself

<details class="check">
<summary>Why take the median of several runs?</summary>

Single runs are noisy (caches, other programs). The median ignores outliers in both directions.

</details>

<details class="check">
<summary>What went wrong with the 'slow in Python' claim?</summary>

It came from reading the code, not from a profile. Measured, the database query dominated; Python was a small share.

</details>
